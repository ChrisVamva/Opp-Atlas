**A3.** Why is "developers aged 25–40" an insufficient audience definition?

**B2.** Headline formula: *Achieve [outcome] without _____.*

## Part C — Hands-on practical (20 pts)

*Use the lesson's cheat sheet only. Paper/doc + one stranger.*

Market a **new** subject (not the lesson's KeyCoach example): a **Notion budgeting template**.

1. **Audience card (8 min):** who (name + role), struggle (in their words), dream outcome, top objection, one watering hole.
2. **Positioning (4 min):** three drafts of the positioning sentence; keep the sharpest.
3. **The page (8 min):** headline (formula), subheadline (for whom + how), 3 benefits (outcome-first, "so what?"-tested), one CTA, one proof element.

**Extension (3 pts):** run the **stranger test**: send headline + subheadline + 3 benefits to one person resembling the audience card. Record their answer to *"What do you think this is, and would you click?"* — and one change you made because of it.

### Rubric
| Points | Standard |
|---|---|
| 12 | All five page jobs present and outcome-first; audience card vivid (a person, not a demographic); positioning sharp |
| 5 | Every benefit survives the "so what?" test; no feature-leads |
| 3 | Extension: stranger test run + revision recorded |

## Part D — Diagnose the mistake (8 pts · bug 2 + fix 2 each)

**D1.** A landing page leads with: "Built with Notion formulas, 200 cells, 12 databases — the ultimate system!" — and converts poorly.

**D2.** A headline reads: "Synergy for your finances." The founder loves it; visitors are confused.

**D3.** *(Bonus scenario, not scored separately)*: the audience card says the audience gathers "on the internet." Why is that useless for the marketing plan?

---

## Part E — Apply & extend (6 pts)

A founder says: "The product is for everyone, so my copy shouldn't narrow it." In **4–6 sentences**, push back using the lesson's ideas — *clarity converts, cleverness decorates*, the audience card, and what "for everyone" copy actually produces.

## Answer key

<details><summary><strong>Part A</strong></summary>

**A1.** Feature = what it is; benefit = what they get. Sell benefits — outcomes are what people buy. · **A2.** *"___ helps ___ achieve ___ without ___."* · **A3.** It's a statistic, not a person — no struggle, no words, no watering holes; you can't write copy for a demographic. · **A4.** Headline (promise), subheadline (for whom/how), 3 benefits, one CTA, one proof element.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part B</strong></summary>

**B1.** so what · **B2.** [pain] · **B3.** CTA (call-to-action) · **B4.** customer
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part C — grading notes</strong></summary>

New subject = transfer of the five-jobs skeleton. Grading looks for: benefit-first bullets that survive "so what?" (e.g., "200 cells" → "see where your money goes in one glance"), a headline following the formula (e.g., "Know where your money goes — without spreadsheets"), and an audience card with a *name and situation*, not a segment. Extension: the recorded confusion is the point — clarity is tested outside your own head.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part D</strong></summary>

**D1.** Bug: feature-lead — the copy is about the *product*, not the buyer. Fix: lead with the outcome ("see where your money goes in one glance"), move specs below the fold. · **D2.** Bug: clever-over-clear — mystery headlines lose to plain outcome statements. Fix: headline formula; use the audience's own words. · **D3.** (bonus) "The internet" isn't a watering hole — the plan needs *specific* places (named subreddits, Discords, newsletters) to mine phrases and reach people.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part E — what a strong answer includes</strong></summary>

"For everyone" produces copy that persuades no one — specificity is what makes a promise land; the audience card exists to sharpen every sentence; clarity converts, cleverness decorates; narrowing the *copy* doesn't exclude buyers, it makes the right ones feel addressed. Optional: start narrow, expand after traction.
</details>
