## Part C — Hands-on practical (20 pts)

*Use the lesson's cheat sheet only. This test ships a real artifact — the rewrite is the grade.*

A customer writes:

> "Your app ate my data. I lost TWO YEARS of notes after the sync bug. My therapist says journaling keeps me sane and now it's all gone. I want everything back or a full refund. This is unacceptable."

Draft your reply in under **130 words**, then answer:

1. **The reply** — full AER structure, plain language, ownership voice, their name (invent one), a concrete next step with a timeline, and empathy naming *their* specific impact.
2. **The anatomy (3 lines):** label which sentences did Acknowledge / Explain / Resolve.

**Extension (3 pts):** add the two most likely follow-up questions you'd expect, with your planned answers (e.g., "how long until I know?") — the start of a template.

### Rubric
| Points | Standard |
|---|---|
| 12 | AER complete and in order; specific empathy; concrete resolve with timeline; under 130 words |
| 5 | No blame (customer or "the system"), no policy-speak, ownership voice throughout |
| 3 | Extension: two follow-ups with answers |

## Part D — Diagnose the mistake (8 pts · bug 2 + fix 2 each)

**D1.** A support agent replies: "As per our policy, data loss due to sync errors is not covered. You should have enabled backups."

**D2.** A reply reads: "We sincerely apologize for the inconvenience. We apologize once more for the inconvenience. Thank you for your patience."

**D3.** *(Bonus scenario, not scored separately)*: an agent resolves the issue brilliantly but never states a next step — the customer replies "so... what now?"

## Part E — Apply & extend (6 pts)

A teammate says: "The customer is just being dramatic — the facts are the facts, I'll fix it and reply with the fix only." In **4–6 sentences**, respond using the lesson's ideas: *feelings first, facts second*, what acknowledgment does to the rest of the message, and what "the fix lands flat until they feel heard" costs the relationship.

## Answer key

<details><summary><strong>Part A</strong></summary>

**A1.** Acknowledge (show you understood the specific impact) → Explain (honest cause, plain words) → Resolve (who does what by when); solutions can't land until people feel heard, so acknowledge first. · **A2.** Empathy names the customer's *specific* situation; over-apology repeats generic sorries that read scripted and insincere. · **A3.** It signals an unpersonalized template — the opposite of feeling heard. · **A4.** "Soon" creates follow-up tickets and anxiety; give dates or honest ranges ("within 2 business days").
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part B</strong></summary>

**B1.** I · **B2.** system · **B3.** inconvenience · **B4.** can
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part C — model answer</strong></summary>

> "Hi Ana — two years of notes, gone, right before you need them: that's devastating, and I'm sorry. I checked your account: last night's sync bug corrupted your local backup — this was our failure, not anything you did. Our engineers restored your note history from server backups overnight; your notes will re-appear by 9:00 tomorrow, and I've emailed you the moment they do. I'll also personally verify three of your oldest notes are intact. If anything's still missing by tomorrow, reply here directly and I'll stay on it until it's fully resolved."

*(~110 words: names her, names the specific impact, honest explanation taking blame off her, who-does-what-by-when, direct line back.)*

Anatomy: Acknowledge = sentence 1; Explain = sentence 2; Resolve = sentences 3–5. Extension follow-ups: "What if my notes don't come back?" → refund offered unprompted; "Will this happen again?" → bug fixed + automatic backups now on.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part D</strong></summary>

**D1.** Bug: policy-speak + blaming the customer ("you should have..."). Fix: acknowledge the impact, explain honestly in human words, resolve with what you *can* do (restore, refund, escalation); translate policy into reasons. · **D2.** Bug: robotic over-apology — scripted sorries, zero specific empathy. Fix: name the actual impact once; generic "inconvenience" three times = zero. · **D3.** (bonus) Resolve requires a next step — who does what by when; a brilliant fix without a stated next step leaves the customer in limbo ("soon" ≠ a step).
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part E — what a strong answer includes</strong></summary>

Customers read feelings first, facts second; acknowledgment opens the door — the identical fix lands warm after it and cold without it; "fix-only" replies read as dismissal and cost trust (the relationship), even when the fix is perfect; AER exists because *order* is part of the message. Optional: dismissal is what customers quote in reviews, not the bug itself.
</details>
