# First Lesson — Keyboard (Touch Typing)

> **Welcome!** Touch typing is typing without looking at the keyboard, using all ten fingers with each finger responsible for its own keys. Today you'll learn the home-row system, measure your current speed, and do your first real drill. This is the rare skill that pays you back every single day for the rest of your life — every email, every message, every line of code.

---

## 1. Why learn this?

- The average hunt-and-peck typist does 30–40 WPM. A trained touch typist does 60–80+ — *double*.
- You stop looking down; your eyes stay on the screen, so your thoughts flow instead of stopping.
- It's pure technique, not talent: anyone who practices 15 minutes a day gets fast.

**Why it's in a collection of technical skills:** every other folder here — [Python](../Python/First%20Lesson.md), [Terminal](../Terminal/First%20Lesson.md), [JavaScript](../JavaScript/First%20Lesson.md) — rewards fast, accurate typing. This is the cheapest speed upgrade you can buy.

## 2. Prerequisites

- A keyboard (laptop keyboards are fine to start).
- A free typing website — any of these works: **keybr.com**, **typing.com**, **TypingClub**, or **monkeytype.com**.
- 15–20 quiet minutes today, and ideally a daily slot this week.

## 4. Core concepts

### 4.1 The home row is where fingers rest
Put your fingers on the middle row of letters:

```
A  S  D  F   ·   J  K  L  ;
left pinky→       ←right pinky
```

Your left index finger sits on **F**, your right index on **J**. Feel those keys: most keyboards have a small bump on F and J — find the bumps without looking. That's your anchor. Every time you finish reaching for a key, fingers *return* to home row, like a boxer returning to guard.

### 4.2 Each finger owns specific keys
No finger crosses over another. The rough map:

- **Left pinky:** A, Q, Z · **Left ring:** S, W, X · **Left middle:** D, E, C · **Left index:** F, G, R, T, V, B
- **Right index:** J, H, U, Y, N, M · **Right middle:** K, I, comma · **Right ring:** L, O, period · **Right pinky:** `;`, P, `/` and Enter/Shift
- **Thumbs:** the space bar, either one.

You don't memorize this map today — the drills teach it to your fingers automatically.

### 4.3 Accuracy beats speed
A fast typist who makes errors is slower than a precise one — every mistake costs a backspace plus a re-type. Rule: **be accurate first; speed arrives on its own.** If you drop below ~95% accuracy in a drill, slow down deliberately.

### 4.4 Look at the screen, not your hands
This will feel slow and clumsy for a few days — that's the feeling of your brain rewiring. Resisting the urge to peek *is* the training.

### 4.5 Posture and setup
- Feet flat, wrists floating (not resting on the desk edge), elbows ~90°.
- Screen at eye level or slightly below.
- If you can touch-type the letters but not symbols (`{`, `[`, `#`), that's a later lesson — for programming, symbols matter a lot and get their own drills in week two.

## 5. Hands-on exercise: baseline → drills → baseline

**Step 1 — Set up.** Open keybr.com (or typing.com). Sit tall, find the F and J bumps, rest fingers on home row.

**Step 2 — Baseline test.** Take a 1-minute test *typing however you normally do*. Write down your **WPM** (words per minute) and **accuracy**. Example: `Baseline: 38 WPM, 91%`. This number is your "before" photo. Don't be embarrassed by it — everyone starts here.

**Step 3 — Home-row drill (10 min).** In your typing site, do the beginner/home-row lessons only. Keep eyes on the screen. When you make a mistake, *don't* flail — pause, find the key blind, continue. Repeat rows like `fff jjj fjf fjf` slowly and evenly until they're smooth.

**Step 4 — Add the top row (10 min).** Continue lessons introducing E, I, R, U. Go slower than feels necessary. Even rhythm — think metronome, not sprint.

**Step 5 — Cool-down sentence (5 min).** Type this exact sentence five times, eyes on screen:

```
the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog
```

It uses every letter. Aim for zero backspaces on the final repetition.

**Step 6 — Second test.** 1-minute test again, proper touch technique. Compare with your baseline. Even if speed is identical or slightly lower today (normal!), accuracy and confidence should improve. Write down the new numbers.

**Expected result:** you know home row by feel, you have two numbers written down, and your fingers have their first map.

## 7. Common beginner mistakes

1. **Looking down "just this once."** Every peek re-trains hunting. Tape a note over your hands if needed — seriously.
2. **Chasing speed immediately.** High speed + low accuracy builds bad habits that are hard to unlearn later.
3. **Skipping rest.** Fingers and wrists need breaks; stop if you feel tingling in your wrists. Never type through wrist pain.
4. **Irregular practice.** Ten minutes daily beats a weekend marathon — spacing is what builds muscle memory.
5. **Practicing only easy words.** Struggle is the workout; mixed lessons on keybr.com adapt to your weak keys automatically.
