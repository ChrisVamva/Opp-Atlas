## Part C — Hands-on practical (20 pts)

*Use the lesson's cheat sheet only. You'll need your typing site (keybr/typing.com/monkeytype).*

1. Sit tall: feet flat, wrists floating, find both anchor bumps **without looking**.
2. Run a **1-minute test** typing *with proper home-row technique only* — no peeking, even once.
3. Record the two numbers that matter: **WPM** and **accuracy %**.
4. Compare against your **baseline** from the First Lesson (e.g., `Baseline: 38 WPM, 91%`).
5. Do one 5-minute home-row + top-row drill at deliberate, even rhythm.
6. Finish by typing `the quick brown fox jumps over the lazy dog` once, aiming for **zero backspaces**.

**Extension (3 pts):** type the pangram again with eyes fully closed. Report what happened (the point: proof the anchor-key system works).

## Answer key

<details><summary><strong>Part A</strong></summary>

**A1.** **F** (left index) and **J** (right index); the small physical bumps on those keycaps. · **A2.** Be accurate first and speed follows; when accuracy drops (below ~95%), deliberately slow down. · **A3.** E: left middle finger (up from D). P: right pinky. · **A4.** Spaced practice builds muscle memory; short daily sessions beat massed weekend marathons.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part B</strong></summary>

**B1.** `J K L ;` · **B2.** return · **B3.** thumbs · **B4.** two
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part C — grading notes</strong></summary>

Two numbers are required (WPM + accuracy) plus the comparison to baseline — progress is *measured*, not felt. The closed-eyes pangram is the "aha": anchors replace sight. Fair scoring cares about technique (eyes up, fingers returning to home row), not the raw number — a 32 WPM with 98% accuracy and honest numbers scores higher than a peeking 55.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part D</strong></summary>

**D1.** Bug: chasing speed let errors creep in — speed built on low accuracy collapses under pressure. Fix: return to deliberate-pace drills targeting ≥95% accuracy; speed will reappear, stable. · **D2.** Bug: peeking on hard keys re-trains hunting for exactly the letters that need the most drilling. Fix: hard-commit to screen-only (tape/note over hands if needed), slow down on problem keys, let the anchors (F/J bumps) do their job.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part E — what a strong answer includes</strong></summary>

Speed is one of three: (1) eyes stay on screen → thoughts flow uninterrupted, no context-switching between screen and keys; (2) fewer errors → less time fixing typos (accuracy beats speed); (3) the daily 15-minute cost is tiny against every email/message/line of code for life. May also cite: it's technique not talent — anyone gets fast.
</details>
