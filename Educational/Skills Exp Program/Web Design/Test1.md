## Part C — Hands-on practical (20 pts)

*Use the lesson's cheat sheet only. Paper or Figma.*

1. **Wireframe a product landing page** (not the personal page from the lesson) with: header (logo left, 3 nav links right), hero (headline, subtext, one button), a 3-card features row, a wide testimonial band, and a footer.
2. **Label every box** with its content ("headline", "CTA", "photo"...) and mark which element is the **one** hero element.
3. **Mark the accent usage**: show exactly which two elements get the accent color — and nothing else.
4. **Annotate hierarchy**: arrows or numbers showing the order a visitor's eye should travel (1 → 2 → 3).

**Extension (3 pts):** write a 2-line diagnosis of the lesson's deliberately-plain mini-app page (from the [JavaScript First Lesson](../JavaScript/First%20Lesson.md)): one strength, one fix — using at least two of the five concepts by name.

## Part D — Diagnose the mistake (8 pts · bug 2 + fix 2 each)

**D1.** A page uses four accent colors (blue buttons, green links, orange headings, red badges) and six fonts. It "looks lively" but amateur.

**D2.** A designer centers every paragraph on the page "for symmetry," and body paragraphs stretch 120+ characters across the screen.

## Answer key

<details><summary><strong>Part A</strong></summary>

**A1.** Layout, visual hierarchy, typography, color, whitespace. · **A2.** Whether structure and hierarchy survive without detail; a blurred mush means hierarchy is missing. · **A3.** 60% dominant neutral (near-white/near-black background), 30% secondary (brand color, section backgrounds), 10% accent (buttons/links). · **A4.** Layout and hierarchy decisions come first — styling is cheaper and better once structure is fixed ("painting a house you haven't built").
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part B</strong></summary>

**B1.** 16 (minimum); ~1.5 · **B2.** 50–75 · **B3.** buttons · **B4.** padding/whitespace
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part C — grading notes</strong></summary>

A new page type (product landing) tests transfer, not memory: same five zones, new arrangement. Grading looks for: labeled boxes, exactly one hero element, accent confined to two elements max, and a legible eye-path. Extension grades vocabulary-in-use ("no hierarchy — the hero doesn't win the squint test", "body text at 14px").
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part D</strong></summary>

**D1.** Bug: accent scarcity broken — if everything shouts, nothing is heard; and >2 font families erodes hierarchy. Fix: one accent color (10%), two fonts max, let size/weight do the differentiating. · **D2.** Bug: centering everything flattens hierarchy (center only heroes); 120-char lines exceed the 50–75 range and tire the eye. Fix: left-align body text, cap line length (max-width).
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part E — what a strong answer includes</strong></summary>

Whitespace is not wasted space — it signals confidence and makes content readable; instead of adding content: increase padding, group related content, strengthen hierarchy so one element dominates, re-run the squint test. Optional killer argument: cramped pages read as cheap; "professional" is spacing and restraint, not volume.
</details>
