## Part C — Hands-on practical (20 pts)

*Use the lesson's cheat sheet only. Paper/doc — thinking work, graded like the lesson's exercise.*

Pick a candidate from your knowledge inventory (new one, not the lesson's example). Then:

1. **Inventory slice (5 min):** list five things you know that took real effort to learn.
2. **Narrow (5 min):** write the full audience sentence for your best candidate — narrow enough to be un-clichéd.
3. **Filter (5 min):** score it 1–5 on pain / credibility / reach / effort, with one justification line per score.
4. **MVP outline (5 min):** outcome promise, one format, 5–7 numbered sections, one bonus.

**Extension (3 pts):** write your exact validation question for 5 named people — the sentence you'd actually send (e.g., "Would you pay $9 for a guide that does X? What's missing?").

| 5 | The MVP delivers *one* outcome in *one* format — no scope creep |

## Part D — Diagnose the mistake (8 pts · bug 2 + fix 2 each)

**D1.** A creator spends six weeks building a course + ebook + template pack for "freelancers," then launches to 0 sales.

**D2.** A creator's product scores pain 5, credibility 5, reach 2, effort 4 — and they build it anyway because "the idea is too good."

**D3.** *(Bonus scenario, not scored separately)*: a learner "validates" by asking friends and family — all say "great idea!" What's wrong with that evidence?

## Part E — Apply & extend (6 pts)

A friend says: "I don't have anything to teach." In **4–6 sentences**, argue otherwise using the lesson's terms: the three wells, *solved problems*, and the beginner-advantage principle ("someone a few steps behind you needs what you just learned").

## Answer key

<details><summary><strong>Part A</strong></summary>

**A1.** Outcomes — saved time, money, competence, calm. The format (PDF/video/template) is just the container. · **A2.** Your own solved problems; questions you keep answering; your unfair advantages. · **A3.** Ask → landing page → pre-sell → build. Pre-selling converts real money into the strongest evidence *before* weeks of build cost. · **A4.** The smallest version that still delivers the **full outcome**; *not* "a sloppy full version" and *not* "half an outcome."
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part B</strong></summary>

**B1.** a specific audience; the outcome they want · **B2.** reach · **B3.** community/distribution (audience location) · **B4.** learn (get feedback)
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part C — grading notes</strong></summary>

New candidate = transfer, not recall. Grading looks for: a genuinely narrow audience sentence (not "freelancers"), per-score justification (especially reach: *where* do these people gather?), and an MVP with exactly one format delivering one outcome — sections must each be a step toward the promise. Extension: the question must be sendable as-is, with a price anchor and an open "what's missing?".
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part D</strong></summary>

**D1.** Bug: broad audience ("everyone" = no one) + multi-format scope creep + no validation. Fix: one narrow audience, one format, validation ladder before building. · **D2.** Bug: distribution ignored — reach 2/5 means you can't find the buyers; the idea can't sell itself. Fix: park it, or find the community first (distribution beats idea quality at the start). · **D3.** (bonus) Polite bias: friends/family aren't the market and won't say no to your face. Real validation asks *strangers in the audience* with a price anchor.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part E — what a strong answer includes</strong></summary>

The three wells always produce something (solved problems, repeated questions, unfair advantages); "a few steps behind you" is exactly who needs it — beginners underestimate what they know; the validation ladder means testing costs nothing before building; first product = priced to learn.
</details>
