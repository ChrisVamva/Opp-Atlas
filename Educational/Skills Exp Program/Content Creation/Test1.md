## Part A — Concept check (8 pts · 2 each)

**A2.** What are the three idea sources, and what do you do the moment an idea appears?

**A3.** What three lines make up the skeleton, and what does each do?

**A4.** Why does the 15-minute draft forbid re-reading?

**B1.** Drafting rule: don't look back, don't fix sentences, just _____ the end.

**B2.** When editing, cut about _____% of the words.

**B3.** The first piece is complete at _____–_____ words.

## Part C — Hands-on practical (20 pts)

*Use the lesson's cheat sheet only. This test ships real content — the artifact is the grade.*

Run the full loop on a **new idea**, compressed into one sitting:

1. **Idea list (5 min):** three fresh ideas, using the three sources (not ones from the lesson).
2. **Pick + skeleton (5 min):** choose the smallest; write Promise / Points / Punch.
3. **Draft (10 min):** timer on, no re-reading, reach the end.
4. **Edit once (5 min):** cut the opening line, cut 20%, sharpen the title to something concrete.
5. **Ship (5 min):** publish it (or send to one human with "honest feedback welcome" — that counts), and record where + how it felt.

**Extension (3 pts):** add the piece to an idea-list/log (Notion database counts — see the [Notion First Lesson](../Notion/First%20Lesson.md)) with date, title, and one lesson-learned line.

| Points | Standard |
|---|---|
| 12 | Full loop executed in order; a real draft edited and actually shipped |
| 5 | The draft obeys the discipline (promise evident, opening cut, concrete title) |
| 3 | Extension logged |

## Answer key

<details><summary><strong>Part A</strong></summary>

**A1.** Idea → outline → draft → edit → publish; drafting and editing must be separated. · **A2.** Problems you solved / questions people asked you / things you changed your mind about; capture it to the idea list instantly, judge never while capturing. · **A3.** Promise (what the reader gets — title + first line), Points (the 2–4 ideas delivering it), Punch (the takeaway/action). · **A4.** Re-reading wakes the inner editor; the goal is a finished bad draft, not a perfect unfinished one.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part B</strong></summary>

**B1.** reach · **B2.** 20 · **B3.** 150–300 · **B4.** weekly (consistently, over time)
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part C — grading notes</strong></summary>

Graded on *discipline*, not prose quality: was the skeleton written before the draft; was the timer honored (no re-reading); was the opening cut and title made concrete; was it actually shipped. The recorded "how it felt" is the quiet lesson — dread is always bigger than the thing.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part D</strong></summary>

**D1.** Bug: drafting and editing at once — perfectionism killed momentum. Fix: timer + no-looking-back draft, edit *after* reaching the end. · **D2.** Bug: judging the loop on one repetition — one post proves nothing; compounding comes from weekly publishing and monthly review. Fix: publish weekly for 8 weeks (the lesson's rule), then double down on what got responses.
</details>

<details><summary><strong>Part E — what a strong answer includes</strong></summary>

"Ready" is a feeling *after* publishing, not before; volume reveals your voice — nobody's first piece is their voice; the idea list means they'll never run out; first pieces are push-ups (practice reps), and the loop (idea → outline → draft → edit → publish) compounds weekly. Optional: niche is *found* by publishing, not decided in advance.
</details>
