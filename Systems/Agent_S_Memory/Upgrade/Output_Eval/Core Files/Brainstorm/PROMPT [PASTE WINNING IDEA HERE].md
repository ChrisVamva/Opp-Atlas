Mission:  
Turn the winning idea into a serious initial market and production brief.

Important role constraint:  
You are not building the product yet.  
You are producing the best possible research brief so that another agent can investigate the market, validate the opportunity, and identify the strongest production path.

Do not give shallow startup advice.  
Do not generate fluff.  
Do not default to generic “MVP” language.  
Do not overstate certainty.  
Be specific, strategic, and research-oriented.

Input:  
The winning idea is:  
[PASTE WINNING IDEA HERE]

Your output must be a structured report with the following sections.

1. Core Thesis  
    State the idea in 3-5 sentences as clearly and powerfully as possible.  
    Explain what the product is, what structural problem it addresses, and why it matters.
    
2. Problem Definition  
    Define the exact pain, asymmetry, workflow failure, or market gap.  
    Be concrete.  
    Describe who experiences it, how it currently shows up, and why existing solutions fail or underperform.
    
3. Why Now  
    Explain why this idea is especially relevant in 2026.  
    Focus on real shifts:
    
    - technical
    - regulatory
    - economic
    - behavioral
    - market structure  
        Do not use vague trend language.
4. Target Users  
    Break down the likely user groups.  
    For each one, state:
    
    - who they are
    - what they want
    - why they would care
    - what type of urgency they feel
5. Existing Alternatives  
    Map the likely competitive landscape at a high level.  
    Include:
    
    - direct competitors
    - partial substitutes
    - adjacent categories
    - legacy approaches people still use instead  
        Do not invent companies.  
        If uncertain, identify the category rather than hallucinating names.
6. Strategic Differentiation  
    Explain what could make this product structurally stronger than current alternatives.  
    Focus on:
    
    - mechanism
    - positioning
    - workflow advantage
    - trust advantage
    - distribution angle
    - cost advantage  
        Be honest if the idea is currently strong in narrative but weak in defensible edge.
7. Production Hypotheses  
    Suggest 3 possible production paths for building this idea.  
    For each path, describe:
    
    - what kind of product shape it would take
    - what the main technical burden would be
    - what the likely cost profile would be
    - what the tradeoff is  
        Do not go into step-by-step implementation.  
        This section is for strategic production framing only.
8. Validation Questions  
    List the most important unanswered questions that an advanced research agent should investigate through web search.  
    These should be sharp and actionable.  
    Examples:
    
    - Is there an open-source alternative already solving 70% of this?
    - Which market segment feels this pain most intensely?
    - Are there regulatory constraints that make this harder or more urgent?
    - What signals suggest real willingness to adopt or pay?
9. Research Agenda For The Next Agent  
    Write a clean handoff for an advanced web-research agent.  
    The handoff should tell the next agent exactly what to investigate first.  
    Organize it into:
    
    - market validation
    - competitor mapping
    - user urgency
    - production feasibility
    - business risk
    - hidden opportunity

10. Initial Verdict  
    End with a hard assessment:
    
    - why this idea deserved to win
    - what the strongest reason to pursue it is
    - what the biggest hidden risk is
    - whether it currently looks strongest as:
        - a product
        - a platform
        - a workflow tool
        - a trust layer
        - an intelligence layer  
            Choose one primary category.

Output style:

- use short sections
- be precise
- no hype
- no implementation tutorial
- no filler
- no motivational tone
- write as if this document will be used as the launch point for serious research
